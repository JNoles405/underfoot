Underfoot

An ant farm between two panes of glass. The colony digs on its own. Drop crumbs on the surface and the workers carry them down to the queen, who lays eggs that hatch into more diggers.

Field notes

  • Packed sand holds. Tunnels keep their shape. Sand you pour is loose until it lands.
  • Two crumbs, one egg. The queen lays when fed. Eggs hatch in about 20 seconds.
  • Water soaks in. Wet sand darkens and slowly dries. Ants that stay under water too long drown.
  • Scent leads to food. An ant carrying a crumb home leaves a violet trail. Workers that cross it follow it back to the food. Trails fade within a minute or two.
  • Ants rest at night. A day lasts four minutes. After dark, idle workers slow down and dig less. Carriers still finish the job.
  • Real speed. A garden ant walks about 2 cm a second. At this farm's scale, 2 mm a cell, that is ten cells a second.
  • The dead are carried out. Workers that die of old age or drown leave their remains. Nestmates haul them up and out onto the surface, where they crumble away in about a day.
  • Workers age. Each lives a few minutes, so keep the crumbs coming.
